# G-Zero

**A tabletop anti-gravity racing game.**

No weapons. No hull points. No shooting. Just speed, corners, G-forces, and the occasional catastrophic blackout.

Six sided dice, movement templates, and a track. Every craft on the grid is identical; the difference is what the pilot is willing to do to their own body to get through the next corner.

---

## What this is

G-Zero began as a hack of [Gaslands: Refuelled](https://ospreypublishing.com/gaslands-refuelled/) by Mike Hutchinson and has since grown its own rules. It keeps the shape of the parent game (the round, the templates, the idea that a movement system can be a pressure system) and replaces everything else.

The design targets are F-Zero, WipeOut, Extreme G, and Speed Racer, dropped into a world where corporations outlived states and racing is the spectacle that keeps the arrangement watchable.

**Three systems carry the game:**

**Velocity Vector (V1-V6)** decides which movement templates you can select, when in the round you act, and how badly a G-Load Check will go. Faster is better until it is not.

**Altitude (Low / Mid / High)** is what G-Zero has instead of weapons. It is declared secretly each phase. Low buys distance, Mid buys Energy, High buys agility, and when two frames meet, the band differential decides who comes out of it worse.

**Energy and G-Force** are the same equation read from both ends. Energy runs the Inertial Dampener; the Dampener is the only thing clearing the G-Force the track keeps putting into the pilot. Fall behind on that and the cockpit goes dark.

Pilots are not equipment. Nerve only ever goes down, and a dead pilot races nothing else, ever.

## Status

**Playable, not finished.** A complete race works end to end. The document is honest about where it is thin.

Every open item carries a labelled callout in the text:

- **PLAYTEST** marks rules that work but are not tuned, with the known risk and the variant to test.
- **IN DEVELOPMENT** marks sections that are incomplete, with a bridge rule for the meantime.

**Blocking standalone play:** the movement template set, base sizes, the Drift point definition, and the High-G legality table are not yet published. Until they are, use the Gaslands: Refuelled templates and read Gear as Vector.

**One remaining dependency on the parent book:** the optional Hype rules point at the Refuelled Audience Votes table. Everything else in G-Zero is self-contained.

A full list of open items is at the end of Part IX.

## Playing it

You need six sided dice, the movement templates, a track with walls and scenery, and one model per pilot on a standard base. Use whatever models you like.

For a first game, run a Single Race with **Ghost Mode** on. Frames cannot collide with each other, which leaves new players free to learn Vector management and template selection before the pack starts hitting them.

## Contributing

Playtest reports are the most useful thing anyone can send. Open an issue with the Vector, the Altitude, and what the table actually did; edge cases in the collision and G-Load rules are where this needs the most eyes.

Corrections, typos, and pull requests are welcome.
