# G-Zero

---

## THE WORLD DID NOT END; THERE WAS NO MONEY TO BE MADE IN THE END

*What happened instead is that the corps outlived the states, the states outlived the idea of states, and then nothing really changed except the letterhead. The Few run the enclaves. The enclaves run the infrastructure. The infrastructure runs on debt, and the debt runs on the rest.*

*For the few, it worked out. Arcologies with vertical gardens and clean water and a real sky over real earth with real plants. Post-scarcity pockets, well-managed, carefully photographed for the feeds. Proof that the system works.*

*For the rest: the sprawl, the hubs, the artificial dirt. High-tech, low-life. The sky above the blocks is the colour of a feed that has nothing to broadcast. Neural implants run on subscription models. Your air filter is a brand name. Your body is a liability you have not finished paying off.*

*Into this, somebody invented the Null Point Tech: zero gravity.*

*The Boards saw what the Colosseum did for Rome and updated the formula. The G-Zero league is clean, beautiful, and Null-Frames are carefully regulated; a pure sport where the best pilots from any hub push the limits of physics in machines that cost more than most districts do. Broadcast simultaneously to every screen in every timezone. The commentators are excellent. The crashes are spectacular. The drama is delightful.*

*Nobody is supposed to die in G-Zero. The insurance premiums alone guarantee the Boards pay attention. That said, blacking out at 1000 km/h with a wall in front of you is not a fun experience, and the waivers pilots sign before their first qualifying lap would take a corporate lawyer three days to read. But a cockpit painted red, perfectly lit; that is what really pulls the numbers.*

*The tracks are everywhere. Some float above the wastelands. Suspended circuits, kilometres of engineered tarmac hanging in the air above the places the Boards decided were not worth fixing. The rest watches from below. The feeds make it beautiful. Sometimes the race is in your backyard; someone could get hit. The feeds make it beautiful.*

*The pilots know what they are. They know what the crowds are for. Some of them mind.*

*Most of them fly anyway, because the alternative is the sprawl, and they are very, very good at this.*

*Speed is the one thing that does not care who owns it.*

> ##### DESIGN INTENT
> G-Zero is a complete game. Everything you need to play is in this document; no other rulebook is required.
>
> The design goal is a pure anti-gravity racing game in the spirit of F-Zero, Extreme G, WipeOut, and Speed Racer. No weapons. No hull points. No shooting. Just speed, corners, G-forces, and the occasional catastrophic blackout.
>
> Pilots die in this game. Not often, and never by accident of an opponent's aim; only ever because someone pushed a body further than a body goes. That distinction matters to the Boards' legal department and to nobody else.
>
> G-Zero began life as a hack of Gaslands by Mike Hutchinson, and the debt is structural: the round, the templates, the dice economy, the whole idea that a movement system can be a pressure system. It has since grown its own rules and no longer needs the parent book. It still owes it everything.

> ##### VIBE & SOUNDTRACK
> F-Zero. WipeOut. Extreme G. Speed Racer.
> Perturbator, Crystal Castles, Leftfield, Aphex Twin.
> If the walls feel closer and the track feels faster, you are in the right headspace.

# HOW TO READ THIS BOOK

G-Zero is written in the order you will play it.

**Part I** covers everything that exists before the flag drops: the frame, the pilot, the two resources, and the track.

**Part II** covers the structure of a round: who acts, when, and in what order.

**Part III** is the heart of the game. It walks through a single activation step by step, and every rule you need mid-corner lives there or is linked from there.

**Part IV** covers what happens when two things occupy the same air.

**Part V** covers consequences: G-Load Checks, tumbles, and the ways a race ends for a pilot.

**Part VI** covers winning.

**Parts VII to IX** are optional layers: variant rules, campaign play, and content still in development.

The **Quick Reference** at the back reproduces every table in play order.

Throughout, *flavour and worldbuilding are set in italics*. Rules text is set normally. Boxed notes are never rules:

| Box | Means |
|:---|:---|
| **DESIGN NOTE** | Why the rule is the way it is. |
| **PLAYTEST** | This rule is not settled. The box names the known risk and the variant to test. |
| **IN DEVELOPMENT** | This section is incomplete. The box names what is missing and how to bridge it. |
| **SURVIVAL TIP** / **TACTICAL NOTE** | Advice for the player, not the referee. |

# PART I: BEFORE THE RACE

---

## WHAT YOU NEED

- A handful of six-sided dice; six per player is comfortable.
- The G-Zero movement templates and burst templates.
- A track: any surface with a defined racing line, walls, and scenery.
- One Null-Frame model per pilot. Use whatever models you like as long as they fit the standard base. *Nobody at the Boards is checking your livery.*
- Markers or dials for Nerve, Velocity Vector, Altitude, and G-Force.
- Something to hide a secret Altitude declaration: a token placed face-down, a slip of paper, or a dial with a cover.

> ##### IN DEVELOPMENT
> **Base sizes and template dimensions are not yet finalised.** See *Movement Templates* in Part III for the current state of the template set.

---

## THE NULL-FRAME

All Null-Frames are identical. Every craft on the grid runs the same induction array, the same thrust package, and the same drive ceiling of **V6**. Variety comes from the Altitude system, your driving decisions, and (eventually) Sponsor perks.

Null-Frames cannot be destroyed by ordinary play. There are no Hull Points, no armour, and no way to shoot a rival off the track. The only ways to leave a race are voluntary retirement, failing an Emergency Dampener Check, or declaring Red Mist.

*Strip a G-Zero craft down to what keeps the pilot alive and you find three things: a zero-gravity induction array, a thrust package, and an Inertial Dampener. Everything else - the bodywork, the fairings, the sponsor livery, the aerodynamic surfaces that do nothing at Mach 1.5 - is architecture for the cameras. The cockpit sits at the exact geometric centre of the Dampener field. The pilot sits at the exact geometric centre of the cockpit. Every engineering decision made in the construction of a Null-Frame works outward from that point. The human is the load-bearing structure. Everything else is fluff.*

---

## THE PILOT: NERVE

**Nerve** is the pilot's stat, not the frame's. It measures how much the nervous system can still take: reaction latency, vestibular integrity, how long the body holds together under load.

Every pilot starts a race at **Nerve 4**.

Nerve does two things:

- It sets how many dice you roll for **Nerve Dice** and for **G-Load Checks**.
- It measures how close the pilot is to the end.

**Nerve is permanently reduced by 1, to a minimum of 1**, each time a pilot Tumbles or declares a Redout. It does not come back during a race. There is no pit lane and no repair step.

A pilot at **Nerve 1**:

- may not declare **Redout**;
- may not declare **Red Mist**;
- must make an **Emergency Dampener Check** before any G-Load Check.

Track Nerve on a dial or with markers. It only ever goes down.

*What the track takes out of a pilot stays out.*

---

## THE TWO RESOURCES

G-Zero uses two trackable resources: one the pilot measures, one the pilot feels.

### ENERGY

**Energy** is generated during your activation and spent during the same activation. It powers the drive and it powers the Inertial Dampener. **Unspent Energy is lost at the end of your activation.** It does not carry over.

Energy is generated by:

- rolling **5 or 6** on a Nerve Die;
- declaring **Mid** altitude;
- certain Burst Template effects and Sponsor perks.

Energy is spent on the **Inertial Dampener**, on **Vector shifts**, and on **Drift** and **Spin**. See *Step 5: Spend Energy* in Part III.

Track Energy with tokens marked with a lightning bolt, or on a small dial.

### G-FORCE

**G-Force tokens** are hazards, not a resource. They accumulate from Vector changes, High-G templates, bad Nerve Dice, Altitude transitions, collisions, and burst templates.

A Null-Frame holding **6 or more G-Force tokens at the end of its activation** must make a **G-Load Check** (see Part V).

The **Inertial Dampener** is the only system that clears G-Force, and it runs on Energy. Without Energy coming in, G-Force builds until the pilot goes dark.

Track G-Force with a dial, dice, or tokens marked **G**.

> ##### NARRATIVE NOTE
> *Energy is surplus the drive routes back through the cockpit when the frame has capacity to spare. Spend it on the Dampener and it buys back a fraction of what the corner took out of you. Spend it on thrust, on a drift line, on a nose pointed somewhere the physics did not intend, and you are borrowing against the same account. Every point of Energy is a choice about which debt you are willing to carry to the next corner.*

---

## THE VELOCITY VECTOR

The **Velocity Vector** (V1 through V6) is your Null-Frame's current thrust band. It determines which movement templates you may select, when in the round you activate, and how hard a G-Load Check will be.

Your Velocity Vector may never exceed **V6** or drop below **V1**.

| Vector | km/h | m/s | Vibe |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| V1 | ~200 | ~55 | *"Pitlane tempo. System rebooting."* |
| V2 | ~350 | ~97 | *"Slow. Relatively speaking."* |
| V3 | ~600 | ~167 | *"Corners start to have opinions."* |
| V4 | ~900 | ~250 | *"Spectators genuinely nervous."* |
| V5 | ~1150 | ~319 | *"Transonic turbulence. Rattling the cage."* |
| V6 | ~1900 | ~528 | *"Mach 1.5+. Beyond the veil. Dampeners screaming."* |

*The fastest possible speed in G-Zero is the Red Mist manoeuvre at V6, which produces a brief burst in excess of 2400 km/h; roughly 666 m/s, or approximately Mach 2.*

*This is not a gear ratio. It is the output state of the drive system, a number on the cockpit display that pilots use to communicate speed in terms the body can understand. The numbers above are approximate.*

*At these velocities, the cornering forces are not uncomfortable. They are lethal. A tight corner at V3 produces G-loads that would destroy an unprotected body in under a second, and at V6 there is no second. The Inertial Dampener is the only reason a pilot at speed is a pilot and not a pressurised liquid.*

---

## THE ALTITUDE SYSTEM

Every Null-Frame flies at one of three **Altitudes**: **Low**, **Mid**, or **High**. Track it with a token or dial alongside your Vector.

Altitude does three jobs:

| Band | Buys You |
|:---|:---|
| **Low** | Distance. A free bonus movement before your template. |
| **Mid** | Energy. Power routed back through the cockpit. |
| **High** | Agility. Template selection as though you were slower than you are. |

Altitude is **declared secretly** at the start of each Vector Phase and **revealed** when you activate or when a collision forces it. It **persists** between activations: you enter your next activation in the band you finished the last one in, and then declare a new one.

Altitude also decides collisions. Two frames in the same band collide fully; two frames in different bands only graze, and one of them comes out of it better (see Part IV).

Full transition costs and effects are in *Step 2: Resolve Altitude Transition* in Part III.

> ##### DESIGN INTENT
> Altitude is what G-Zero has instead of weapons. Nobody shoots anybody. Pilots fight by choosing a band and betting that their rival chose the wrong one, and the physics of a differential at closing speed does the rest.

---

## THE TRACK

Set up a circuit with **Gates** placed at regular intervals around the racing line. Null-Frames must pass through each Gate in order; passing a Gate out of sequence does not count.

A standard race is **3 laps**. The first Null-Frame to complete all laps and cross the Finish Gate wins.

### SCENERY

Every piece of scenery on the track is classified before the race starts, as one of three object types:

| Object | Description |
|:---|:---|
| **Soft** | Crowd barriers, signage, light debris |
| **Medium Solid** | Billboards, barrels, crates, small containers |
| **Heavy Solid** | Walls, hard gates, pillars, large containers |

Each destructible object is also assigned a **material** for its Burst Template effect (see Part IV).

### ALTITUDE GATES

Some tracks feature **Altitude Gates**: a Gate marked with a required Altitude (Low, Mid, or High). A pilot must be at the marked Altitude when passing through the Gate for the passage to count. Passing at the wrong Altitude does not count and does not grant the Gate; the pilot is not otherwise penalised, beyond having to come back around.

Mark Altitude Gates with a coloured token on the Gate.

### STARTING GRID

Roll off to determine starting positions. Place Null-Frames in a single-file line at the Start Gate, separated by one Null-Frame length.

All Null-Frames begin the race at:

- **V1**
- **Altitude Mid**
- **Nerve 4**
- No G-Force tokens
- No Recovery
- No Emergency Dampener Checks logged

The pilot in first position holds the **Leading Position** for the first round.

# PART II: THE ROUND

---

## THE ROUND SEQUENCE

A round of G-Zero runs as follows:

1. **Vector Phase 1** begins. All players with a qualified vehicle secretly declare Altitude. Players then activate in initiative order.
2. **Vector Phases 2 through 6** follow, each resolved the same way.
3. **End of round.** Remove temporary Burst Templates. Determine the new Leading Position.

A vehicle is **qualified** for a Vector Phase if its current Velocity Vector is **equal to or higher than** the Phase number.

> ##### NOTE
> A frame at V4 is therefore qualified in Phases 1, 2, 3, and 4; but it only activates once per round. It activates in the Phase matching its Vector unless it is delayed. Faster frames act later in the round, which means they act with more information and less room to change their minds.

If a vehicle changes Vector during its activation, it activates in its **new** Vector Phase next round.

---

## INITIATIVE ORDER

**Initiative order is the current race standing, from leader to last place.**

Race standing is measured by:

1. Gates passed (more is better);
2. Distance to the next Gate (shorter is better);
3. If two frames are still exactly tied, roll off; the winner takes the higher position.

The player holding the **Leading Position** activates first in each Vector Phase. Play then proceeds down the standings.

**Leading Position passes to the player whose vehicle is currently leading the race** at the end of each round.

Within each Vector Phase, when it is your turn you must either:

- activate one of your qualified vehicles, or
- declare a **Delayed Activation**.

A player with multiple qualified vehicles may freely choose which to activate.

**You may not pass your turn.** If you have a qualified vehicle, you act. The only ways to influence when you act relative to others are your Vector choice between rounds and Delayed Activation within a Vector Phase.

---

## SECRET ALTITUDE DECLARATION

At the **start of each Vector Phase**, every player with a qualified vehicle secretly notes their chosen Altitude for this activation (Low, Mid, or High), face-down.

**Altitude is revealed when:**

- it is that player's turn to activate, or their Delayed Activation resolves; or
- their Null-Frame is involved in a collision before their activation, in which case both pilots involved immediately reveal their Altitude to resolve the collision.

Once revealed, Altitude is public for the remainder of that activation and persists until the pilot's next activation.

**Altitude cannot be changed in response to anything.** It is locked at the start of the phase.

---

## DELAYED ACTIVATION

When it is your turn to activate, instead of moving immediately you may declare a **Delayed Activation**:

1. **Reveal your Altitude.** Turn your Altitude token face-up. It stays public for the rest of the phase, exactly as if you had activated normally.
2. **Secretly choose your movement template now.** Note it down, or set the physical template aside face-down.
3. **Name a target vehicle.** You may target any qualified vehicle whose controller has *lower* initiative than you; that is, anyone further from the lead in the current standings.
4. The Vector Phase continues with other players activating in order.
5. When your target completes its movement, your Delayed Activation resolves **immediately afterwards**, before any cleanup effects from the target's activation are processed. A G-Load Check is a cleanup effect.
6. Reveal your template and resolve your activation as normal.
7. Then resolve the target's cleanup, including any G-Force it gained.

**Edge cases:**

- If your target declares its own Delayed Activation, yours resolves after their *actual movement*, not after their declaration.
- If your target is removed from the race before activating, your Delayed Activation resolves at the end of the current Vector Phase, using the template you chose.

> ##### TACTICAL NOTE
> Delaying is the leader's tool, and it is a trade, not a gift. You buy the sight of your rivals committing, and you pay for it by broadcasting your Altitude a full activation early. Your Vector is already public, so the moment your band is known, everyone downfield can work out exactly how far you can reach and exactly how much a graze will cost. What they cannot work out is where you are going.
>
> They also cannot change their own Altitude in response; that was locked in at the start of the phase. All they can do is steer. Announcing High is a threat that works whether or not you follow through, and a rival who burns their template getting out of a corridor you never intended to fly has lost the corner anyway. Announcing Low is the opposite: it buys you a long bonus run and tells the entire pack that you are, for one activation, the softest thing on the track.

# PART III: THE ACTIVATION

---

## THE ACTIVATION SEQUENCE

Every activation resolves in this order. Do not reorder these steps.

| Step | Action |
|:---:|:---|
| **1** | **Declarations.** Redout, Red Mist, Slipstream. |
| **2** | **Reveal Altitude and resolve the transition.** Bonus movement, Energy, or template shift. |
| **3** | **Select and place your movement template.** Pay any High-G cost. |
| **4** | **Roll Nerve Dice.** |
| **5** | **Spend Energy.** Dampener, Vector shift, Drift, Spin. |
| **6** | **Move and resolve collisions.** |
| **7** | **End of activation.** Lose unspent Energy. G-Load Check if at 6+ G. |

> ##### DESIGN NOTE
> Steps 5 and 6 are deliberately in this order. You commit to a template, learn what the frame did to your body, then decide how to finish the line; only then does the frame actually travel it. This means Drift and Spin are bought *before* you know whether they will hit anybody, which is the point. If your table prefers resolving movement before Energy spending, that also works, but Drift and Spin stop being gambles.

---

## STEP 1: DECLARATIONS

Anything declared at the start of an activation is declared now, before Altitude is revealed. This covers **Redout** and **Red Mist** (below), and the optional **Slipstream** rule (Part VII).

### REDOUT

*A redout occurs when a pilot exceeds physiological limits during high negative-G manoeuvres. The force drives blood into the head, and the visual field takes on a distinct reddish hue.*

**Redout:** At the start of your activation, you may declare a Redout. Permanently reduce your Nerve by 1 (minimum 1). Until the end of your activation, you may select any movement template regardless of your current Velocity Vector, ignoring all Vector restrictions. G-Force costs are paid as normal.

You may declare Redout as many times across a race as you like. Each declaration costs another point of Nerve.

**A pilot at Nerve 1 cannot declare a Redout.**

> ##### SURVIVAL TIP
> Redout is what you reach for in the final lap when your only path to a podium is doing things your Null-Frame was not designed for. Stacking Redouts is what you reach for when you have already decided you are going to win or detonate.

### RED MIST

*The Inertial Dampener keeps a pilot alive. This is not a metaphor; at V5 and above, switching it off is a death sentence measured in milliseconds. Red Mist is the deliberate choice to stop managing that equation: to route everything into thrust, hold the nose straight, and let the G-forces finish what they started.*

*Some pilots reach this state by accident. The legendary ones reach it on purpose, on the final straight, with their nose pointed at the Finish Gate.*

**Red Mist:** Once per race, at the start of one of your activations, and only if you are currently at **V5 or V6**.

- Add **two Long Straight templates**, in any order, to your normal template for this activation.
- You roll **no Nerve Dice** and gain **no G-Force** from the Red Mist movement itself. *The Dampener is offline; there is nothing left to measure.*
- Collisions along your path are resolved normally for the other Null-Frames involved. You yourself ignore all G-Force from those collisions.
- **At the end of this activation, regardless of your position on the track, your pilot dies.** The frame is removed and is permanently lost.

**Exception:** if you crossed the Finish Gate on your final lap during this activation, you are recorded as a **Finisher**. The result book will list your placing.

**A pilot at Nerve 1 cannot declare Red Mist.**

*Whether the obituary calls you a winner depends on where the other Null-Frames are.*

---

## STEP 2: RESOLVE ALTITUDE TRANSITION

Reveal your declared Altitude, then apply the transition effect. **The effect depends on the band you are coming from.**

### GOING LOW

You gain a free **bonus movement**, placed from your current position *before* your normal template. Resolve any collisions caused by the bonus movement immediately.

| From | Bonus Move | Cost |
|:---|:---:|:---:|
| Low | Short Straight | free |
| Mid | Medium Straight | 1 G |
| High | Long Straight | 2 G |

*Low always costs something. There is no such thing as a comfortable pass through the boundary layer.*

### GOING MID

No bonus movement, no template shift; just power routed back through the cockpit.

| From | Bonus |
|:---|:---:|
| Mid | 1 Energy |
| Low or High | 2 Energy |

This Energy is added to whatever your Nerve Dice produce and is spent the same way. Like all Energy, it is lost at the end of your activation.

> ##### SURVIVAL TIP
> Mid is where you go to clean up. After a dive or a hard High run you will be sitting on tokens you cannot afford, and Mid is the only band that hands you the means to clear them. Learn to use it as a planned reset rather than a place you end up.

### GOING HIGH

You may select movement templates as though your Vector were **lower than it actually is**. Your real Vector does not change; only your template choice does.

| From | First Step Down | Each Further Step |
|:---|:---:|:---:|
| High | free | 1 G |
| Mid | 1 G | 1 G |
| Low | 2 G | 1 G |

Any High-G cost on the template you finally select is paid as normal, on top of this.

> ##### EXAMPLE
> You are at V6 and were already High. You declare High again. The first step down is free, putting you at V5 for template purposes. You pay 2 more G to reach V3, and select a V3 template. If that template is High-G at V3, you pay that as well.

---

## STEP 3: SELECT AND PLACE YOUR TEMPLATE

Select a movement template legal for your current Velocity Vector (as modified by Going High or Redout) and place it against the front edge of your base.

- **Higher Vectors** give access to longer, faster templates but restrict turning.
- **Lower Vectors** allow tighter turns.
- Some template and Vector combinations are **High-G**: placing the template grants **1 G-Force token immediately**, before dice are rolled.
- Some combinations are **Self Balancing**: they grant **1 free Energy** without rolling. You may still roll Nerve Dice if you wish and add the free Energy to the result.

### MOVEMENT TEMPLATES

**G-Zero currently uses the Gaslands: Refuelled movement templates and its gear/template chart.** Read that chart with the following substitutions and it works as written:

| On the Refuelled chart | In G-Zero |
|:---|:---|
| **Gear** | **Velocity Vector.** Gear 1 is V1, Gear 6 is V6. |
| **Hazardous** | **High-G.** Placing the template grants 1 G-Force token immediately, before dice are rolled. |
| **Shift** | **Self Balancing.** The template grants 1 free Energy without rolling. |
| **Hazard token** | **G-Force token.** |

Everything else on the chart, which templates are legal at which gear and how each one is placed, is used unchanged.

> ##### IN DEVELOPMENT> The Shift mapping is closer than it looks. In Refuelled a Shift cell hands you a gear change; in G-Zero it hands you the Energy that buys a Vector change. You still get the shift, you just get to decide whether you would rather spend it on the Dampener.
> The G-Zero chart does not have to match the Refuelled one. Vector bands are wider than gears, Altitude already supplies a lot of the cornering flexibility that Refuelled puts in the low gears, and the Drift point is a new geometry problem. Treat the substitution table above as a working bridge, not a target.

---

## STEP 4: ROLL NERVE DICE

After placing your template, roll a number of six-sided dice **up to your current Nerve**. These are your **Nerve Dice**. You may roll fewer, or none at all.

| Roll | Result |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 - 2 | **G-Force.** Gain 1 G-Force token. |
| 3 - 4 | Nothing. *The system holds.* |
| 5 - 6 | **Energy.** Bank it for this activation. |

G-Force from a die is gained immediately and cannot be refused. Energy exists only until the end of your activation.

**You get one roll.** There is no re-roll and no second look there is no time for secound guessing at 1000 km/h.

*Rolling is optional because rolling is a gamble. On four dice you will usually come out level, but "usually" is not a thing a body can rely on. A pilot sitting on 5 G-Force tokens who rolls nothing keeps their frame; a pilot who rolls for one more point of Energy may not.*

---

## STEP 5: SPEND ENERGY

Spend Energy in any order, as many times as you can pay for.

| Cost | Action |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | **Inertial Dampener.** Remove 1 G-Force token. |
| 1 | **Shift Vector.** Change your Velocity Vector by one step. Each additional step in the same shift costs 1 G-Force token. |
| 1 + 1 G | **Drift.** Exit at the Drift point on your template instead of at its end. |
| 1 + 1 G | **Spin.** Pivot up to 90 degrees at your final position. |

Rules that apply to all Energy spending:

- Your Velocity Vector may never exceed V6 or drop below V1.
- Actions that cost G-Force cost it whether or not you can afford to clear it afterwards.
- Taking the same action twice costs full price twice and grants no additional effect beyond the second G-Force token.

> ##### PLAYTEST
> **Every Vector change costs Energy. This is the open question in the current draft.**
>
> Earlier text allowed a free +1 / -1 Vector change each activation, inherited from Gaslands gear shifting, while the Energy table charged for it. Both cannot be true. The paid version is written into the rules because it makes the Dampener a real decision: with free shifts, Energy has essentially one use and Step 5 stops being interesting.
>
> The cost is that acceleration now competes directly with survival, which slows the whole grid down and may make V5 and V6 rarer than intended. **Test the free variant** by allowing one free +1 / -1 shift per activation, with Energy still required for any further step. If races feel sluggish or the pack never reaches top speed, that is the fix.

---

## STEP 6: MOVE AND RESOLVE COLLISIONS

Move your Null-Frame along the placed template to its exit point (or to its Drift point, if you bought Drift), then apply any Spin.

If the frame's base contacts another frame or an object during this movement, stop and resolve the collision (Part IV), then continue the movement if the frame is still able to.

### SONIC BOOM

*When a pilot switches to V6, their vehicle breaks the sound barrier. The resulting shockwave is not optional and cannot be suppressed. Physics does not negotiate.*

**On the first activation after switching to V6**, the active player must place a **Sonic Boom Shockwave**: a Large Burst Template placed directly behind the vehicle, oriented along its direction of travel.

The active player chooses whether to place it:

- at the **start of movement**, before the normal template is placed; or
- at the **end of movement**, after the frame has reached its final position.

The template is removed at the **end of the current Vector Phase**.

Effects on other Null-Frames:

- **A Null-Frame that activates while any part of its base overlaps the Shockwave** rolls additional Nerve Dice equal to its current Nerve before resolving its own movement. All results apply normally.
- **A Null-Frame that ends its activation with any part of its base overlapping the Shockwave** immediately rolls Nerve Dice equal to its current Nerve. All results apply normally.

The Null-Frame that generated the Shockwave is not affected by its own template.

> ##### DESIGN NOTE
> This mechanic may need tuning. The intent is that V6 is genuinely violent, not just fast, and that a tight pack running at top speed produces real chaos rather than a clean speed advantage for the leader. Whether a full Nerve's worth of extra dice is the right number, and whether start-of-move versus end-of-move placement produces interesting decisions or just a formality, needs table time. Adjust template size and dice count freely based on how brutal you want V6 to feel.

---

## STEP 7: END OF ACTIVATION

In this order:

1. **All unspent Energy is lost.**
2. If your Null-Frame holds **6 or more G-Force tokens**, make a **G-Load Check** (Part V).

Your activation is over.

---

# PART IV: COLLISIONS

---

*There is no shooting in G-Zero and no damage. A collision costs G-Force, facing, and whatever line you were holding.*

## COLLISION DICE

Every collision is resolved with **Collision Dice**: six-sided dice on which **4, 5, or 6 is a success** and 1, 2, or 3 is nothing.

Each success is spent according to the Reaction you declared: either to inflict G-Force on the other party, or to defend against it.

The frame whose movement caused the contact is the **Aggressor**. The other party is the **Defender**. **In a collision with an object, the object is always the Aggressor.**

---

## COLLISION SEQUENCE

1. **Altitude Check.** Any pilot involved who has not yet revealed their Altitude reveals it now. Compare bands.
2. **Determine pools.** Graze or Full Collision.
3. **Declare Reactions.** Both parties declare before any dice are rolled.
4. **Roll and apply.**
5. **Burst Templates**, if an object was destroyed.

---

## STEP 1: ALTITUDE CHECK

**Same band: Full Collision.** *Both frames are in the same air.*

**Different bands: Graze.** *The frames clip each other.* One comes out of it better, and it is not decided by who was moving:

| Band | Beats | Loses To |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **High** | Mid | Low |
| **Mid** | Low | High |
| **Low** | High | Mid |

In a Graze, the frame that **wins** the matchup rolls **3 Collision Dice**. The frame that **loses** rolls **1**.

---

## STEP 2: FULL COLLISION POOLS

In a Full Collision, both parties roll the same pool. Determine orientation from where the contact landed on the frame that was **struck**:

| Orientation | Point of Contact | Collision Dice |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| **Head-On** | Front edge or corner | Both Vectors added together; minimum 3 |
| **Broadside** | Side edge | The higher of the two Vectors; minimum 2 |
| **Tailgate** | Rear edge or corner | The difference between the Vectors; minimum 1 |

If both frames made contact on a front edge or corner, it is **Head-On for both**.

*Tailgate is the collision of a pack running together: two frames at the same Vector barely touch, because relative to each other they are barely moving. Head-On is the arithmetic of two closing speeds, and it does not forgive anybody.*

---

## STEP 3: REACTIONS

Both parties declare a Reaction before any dice are rolled.

| Reaction | Who May Declare | Effect of Each Success |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Brace** | Anyone | Inflict 1 G-Force token on the other party and pivot them 30 degrees in a direction of your choosing. |
| **Evade** | Defender only | Cancel 1 G-Force token that would be inflicted on you this collision. |

**The Aggressor cannot Evade.** *They committed to the line; the line is what happened.*

Cancelled G-Force is not inflicted, so it also produces no pivot. A Defender who Evades well is not thrown at all.

---

## OBJECTS

Objects do not choose and do not manoeuvre.

- They always **Brace** and never Evade.
- Every one of their Collision Dice is **automatically a success**; there is no roll to make.
- The frame that struck them is always the **Defender** and may Evade.
- **Orientation does not apply.** A stationary object is hit at your full closing speed no matter which face of your frame reaches it first.

| Object | Collision Dice | Debris |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Soft** | 1 | Small Burst |
| **Medium Solid** | Your Vector | Large Burst |
| **Heavy Solid** | Twice your Vector | Extra-Large Burst |

After the collision resolves:

- **Soft** objects are destroyed and removed; your movement continues to its full length.
- **Medium Solid** objects are destroyed and removed; your Vector drops by 1.
- **Heavy Solid** objects do not move, do not break, and do not care. Your movement stops at the point of contact.

---

## BURST TEMPLATES

When a Null-Frame destroys or damages an object, place the appropriate Burst Template (Small, Medium, Large, or Extra-Large) centred on the point of collision, oriented along the colliding vehicle's direction of travel.

**Any other vehicle whose base overlaps the Burst Template** suffers the effect determined by the object's **material**. The colliding vehicle itself is not affected by its own Burst Template; it has already paid through the collision.

**Duration:**

- **Temporary** templates persist for the remainder of the current round and are removed at end of round.
- **Permanent** hazards remain in place until the referee removes them.

Assign each destructible object a material at track setup.

*At 900 km/h, splinters and shrapnel hang in the air for only a few seconds; but that is long enough for every Null-Frame behind to run through them.*

### BURST TEMPLATE EFFECTS

| Material | Template | Duration | Effect |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **Liquid** (coolant, fuel, organics) | Small | Temporary | Roll 1 fewer Nerve Die on your next activation. *The cockpit feed does not need to explain what it was.* |
| **Debris** (glass, light panels) | Small | Temporary | +1 G |
| **Rubble** (structural fragments) | Large | Temporary | +2 G |
| **Wreckage** (collapsed sections, large containers) | Large | Permanent | +2 G; debris remains as a Medium Solid Object |
| **Gas** (pressurised canisters, chemical cloud) | Large | 2 rounds | Roll 1D6 per activation ending in the template: 1-3 gain that many G; 4-6 remove (result - 3) G |
| **Arc** (high-voltage infrastructure, power relays) | Medium | Temporary | +2 G; Dampener disrupted - Energy from your next Nerve Dice roll may not be spent on the Inertial Dampener |
| **Accelerator Field - Short** | Small | Permanent | Bonus Short Straight movement; +1 G |
| **Accelerator Field - Medium** | Medium | Permanent | Bonus Medium Straight movement; +2 G |
| **Accelerator Field - Long** | Large | Permanent | Bonus Long Straight movement; +3 G |
| **Brake Field - Short** | Small | Permanent | Forced -1 Vector immediately |
| **Brake Field - Medium** | Medium | Permanent | Forced -2 Vector immediately |
| **Brake Field - Long** | Large | Permanent | Forced -3 Vector immediately; remove 1 G free |
| **Energy Transfer - Small** | Small | Temporary | +1 Energy immediately |
| **Energy Transfer - Medium** | Medium | Temporary | +2 Energy immediately |
| **Energy Transfer - Large** | Large | Temporary | +3 Energy immediately |

Accelerator and Brake Fields are colour-coded at track setup and may also be placed as permanent track features independent of any collision.

> ##### DESIGN NOTE
> Burst Templates exist to make object collisions matter beyond a single Null-Frame's misfortune. If a leader takes out a stack of barrels on the apex of a corner, the chasers entering that apex deal with the consequences too. Place soft scenery where you want the track to forgive; place heavy scenery where you want it to bite.
>
> The **Gas** entry is the least tested line in this table and the only one with a random swing. If it plays badly, replace it with a flat +2 G.

# PART V: CONSEQUENCES

---

## G-LOAD CHECK

At the end of any activation in which your Null-Frame holds **6 or more G-Force tokens**, you must make a **G-Load Check**.

**If the pilot is at Nerve 1, make an Emergency Dampener Check first.** If they fail it, they die; the frame becomes a Dead Stick and nothing below applies.

Roll a number of dice equal to your current **Nerve**, minus one die for each G-Force token **beyond the sixth**. Each die that rolls **equal to or above your current Velocity Vector** is a success.

| Successes | Result | Effect |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| **3+** | **NERVES HOLD** | Keep your Vector, your Altitude, and your next activation. *The body absorbed it.* |
| **2** | **NERVES FRAY** | **Tumble.** Lose 1 Nerve. Keep your Vector; *the frame is flung, but the drive never faltered.* |
| **1** | **GREYOUT** | **Override.** Vector resets to V1, Altitude resets to Mid. No Nerve lost. |
| **0** | **BLACKOUT** | **Tumble and Override.** Lose 1 Nerve. Vector resets to V1, Altitude resets to Mid. |

**Discard all G-Force tokens in every case.**

*Redout, Greyout, Blackout: the three ways a body under load stops reporting to the pilot. Two of them are on this table. The third is something a pilot chooses.*

> ##### NOTE
> Read the table twice and you will find the shape of a career in it. A pilot at Nerve 4 can still walk away clean. At Nerve 3 they can, barely. At Nerve 2 the top row is arithmetically out of reach and the best available outcome is being thrown across the track. At Nerve 1 there is no outcome left that does not begin with a check to see whether they are still alive.
>
> Vector cuts the same way. At V1 every die is a success and a G-Load Check is an inconvenience. At V6 only a 6 counts, and the dice are not generous with sixes. This is not a rule punishing speed. It is speed.

> ##### PLAYTEST
> **This check is deliberately brutal and has not been tuned.** Two known consequences:
>
> At **V6** with 4 dice, the chance of NERVES HOLD is roughly 1.6 percent. A pilot who reaches 6 G-Force at top speed is almost certainly going to Tumble or go dark. That may be exactly right; V6 is supposed to be a place you visit, not live.
>
> From **Nerve 2 downward**, NERVES HOLD is arithmetically unreachable. The death spiral is intentional, but confirm at the table that it bites at the right moment rather than ending races early.
>
> If it needs softening, the least invasive fix is to let a die count as a success on **5+ at V5 and V6** rather than requiring the exact Vector number. Do not change the 3 / 2 / 1 / 0 thresholds; the whole table is built on them.

---

## TUMBLE

*A Tumble at V1 is embarrassing. A Tumble at V6 is a lesson in high-energy physics.*

Tumble is triggered by a **NERVES FRAY** or **BLACKOUT** result on a G-Load Check, by certain Burst Templates, and by any other effect that names it. The procedure is always the same.

**1. Direction.** A Tumble goes wherever the energy that caused it was already going. Work outward from the cause:

- **Caused by a collision:** the frame is thrown directly away from the point of contact, along the line running from that point through the centre of its own base.
- **Caused by a Burst Template:** the frame is thrown directly away from the centre of the template.
- **No directional cause** (a G-Load Check, or any effect that simply names Tumble): roll 2D6 and read the direction relative to the frame's current heading.

| 2D6 | Direction |
|:---:|:---|
| 2 | Straight back |
| 3 | The nearest rival pilot chooses the direction |
| 4 | Back left |
| 5 | Left |
| 6 | Forward left |
| 7 | Straight forward |
| 8 | Forward right |
| 9 | Right |
| 10 | Back right |
| 11 | **Catastrophic.** Double the Tumble distance and roll again on this table. |
| 12 | You choose the direction |

*Momentum has opinions. Forty-four percent of the time the frame keeps going roughly where it was already going, because that is what mass does. The rest is a body that has stopped steering.*

**Catastrophic Tumbles** stack: each 11 adds one more template of the size appropriate to your Vector, placed end to end. The first 11 therefore doubles the Tumble. The direction is taken from the first result that is not an 11, and every template is placed along it. There is no cap.

A Tumble crosses Gates normally, subject to the usual sequencing rule: a Gate passed out of order does not count, whether the pilot was conscious for it or not. Tumble at V5 or V6, roll an 11, then roll a 12, and cross the Finish Gate sideways at the end of two Long Straights. The result book has no column for how. A win is a win.


**2. Flip.** The frame is thrown along that direction, ignoring everything in its path. Use the template matching the Vector it held when the Tumble triggered:

| Vector at Trigger | Tumble Template |
|:---|:---:|
| V1 - V2 | Short Straight |
| V3 - V4 | Medium Straight |
| V5 - V6 | Long Straight |

**3. Debris Path.** Every other Null-Frame whose base is overlapped by the template is caught in the wreckage and gains G-Force tokens equal to the Tumble length: **1** for Short, **2** for Medium, **3** for Long. There is no reaction, no Altitude comparison, and no dodge; *the frame is through them before the pilot registers it.*

**4. Lose the Nose.** The pilot of the nearest rival Null-Frame pivots the tumbling frame to face any direction they choose.

**5. Aftermath.** The tumbling pilot permanently loses **1 Nerve** for the remainder of the race, to a minimum of 1.

A Tumble does not stop short and does not deflect. *If the path ends against a wall, that is a wall; the frame is there now. Walls do not gain G-Force tokens. They have other ways of objecting.*

---

## OVERRIDE: RECOVERY

**Override** is the system to enable **Recovery** if the pilot is unable to do it themselves.

Override is triggered by a **GREYOUT** or **BLACKOUT** result on a G-Load Check. When it triggers, the autopilot takes the shell and the Null-Frame enters **Recovery**.

A Null-Frame **in Recovery skips its next qualifying activation**. Mark it with a Recovery token and remove the token as that activation is skipped.

**Recovery does not stack.** A frame already in Recovery that triggers Override again gains nothing further.

*When a pilot goes dark, the shell does not stop. It cannot; at these speeds an uncontrolled frame is a safety risk. Every Null-Frame carries an Override protocol, a limited autopilot that engages the moment cockpit biosigns drop below threshold. It holds the last declared vector, avoids the worst of the walls, and keeps the shell in the race until the pilot comes back online.*

*The Boards have never officially acknowledged that Override exists. Autopilots are not dramatic; nobody watches a machine keep itself alive. The commentators call it Recovery, which is a word about the pilot, and that is the whole point. Remote control was proposed once, in a closed session, and the person who proposed it has not proposed anything since.*

---

## EMERGENCY DAMPENER CHECK

When a pilot at **Nerve 1** would make a G-Load Check, they roll 1D6 first. Track how many of these checks the pilot has been forced to make this race.

| Check | Survive on |
|:---:|:---:|
| 1st | 2+ |
| 2nd | 4+ |
| 3rd | 6 |
| 4th | no roll; the pilot dies |

**Success:** make the G-Load Check as normal. A Tumble from it costs no further Nerve; there is none left to lose.

**Failure:** the pilot dies and the Null-Frame becomes a **Dead Stick**. Make no G-Load Check.

> ##### NOTE
> The Emergency Dampener Check is the only thing in ordinary play that kills a pilot who did not choose it. It is the cumulative bill the body has been keeping. The first time it triggers, the pilot almost always lives. The fourth time there is no roll and no chance, just a name added to the other side of the lap sheet.

---

## DEAD STICK

When a pilot dies, the frame is not removed.

At the start of each Vector Phase matching its **last active Vector**, a Dead Stick activates automatically:

- It moves one **Long Straight** template directly forward.
- It rolls no dice, spends nothing, and gains nothing.
- It has no pilot, no Altitude, and no Dampener.
- Gates it passes do not count.

Other Null-Frames resolve collisions with a Dead Stick as normal for themselves; treat the Dead Stick's Altitude as matching whatever it strikes. The Dead Stick ignores all G-Force, all reactions, and all consequences. *Nobody is left to react.*

When a Dead Stick strikes a wall or Heavy Solid Object, roll 1D6:

| Roll | Result |
|:---:|:---|
| **1** | *The Emergency Dampeners fire their last function.* The frame bleeds velocity and stops. Remove it. |
| **2 - 5** | It bounces. The nearest rival pilot pivots it to any facing; it continues from there. |
| **6** | Total structural failure. Remove the frame and place a Large Burst Template at the point of impact. |

A Dead Stick that leaves the track boundary is removed immediately.

*Red Mist is a deliberate sacrifice: an ace burning out on the final straight with the nose pointed at the Finish Gate. This is not that. A pilot who dies to a systems failure leaves behind expensive debris and a frame the track will finish off in its own time. One is a legend written in fire. The other is an Alloy Pizza that has not happened yet.*

# PART VI: WINNING

---

## THE FINISH

The first Null-Frame to pass through the Finish Gate having completed all laps in Gate order wins the race.

Positions behind first are recorded in order of finishing, then by race standing for frames still on the track when the race ends.

A pilot who declared **Red Mist** and crossed the Finish Gate on their final lap during that activation is recorded as a **Finisher** and placed accordingly, despite being dead.

## RETIREMENT

At the start of any of your activations, you may retire. Remove the Null-Frame from the track. The pilot is recorded below all Finishers, in the order they retired, latest first.

*Retiring is the only way out of a G-Zero race that costs a pilot nothing but the result.*

## BEING LAPPED

If a Null-Frame falls more than one full lap behind the leader, its controller may use the **Restart Rule**.

- The Null-Frame is placed at the last Gate legally passed by the **next-placed Null-Frame**, the one directly ahead of it in the current standings. Its Gate sequence and lap count are set to match that Gate.
- It resets to **V1**, **Altitude Mid**, and **0 G-Force tokens**.
- **Nerve, Recovery, and Emergency Dampener Check counts are NOT reset.**

**A Null-Frame that has used the Restart Rule can no longer win.** It is recorded as *Restarted*, listed below every Finisher, and scores no points in Tournament or Season play.

It still races. It still collides, still loses Nerve, and can still kill its pilot.

*The Boards do not switch a camera off because a pilot is out of contention. A frame that cannot win is still forty minutes of broadcast, and a frame that cannot win has nothing left to protect. The commentators are not supposed to use the word "restarted" on air. They have been told it tests badly.*

> ##### DESIGN NOTE
> Restarting is a trade the player makes with themselves, and it should stay optional.  A player who is out of contention on lap one should be allowed to spend the rest of the evening causing problems for people who are not.**

# PART VII: OPTIONAL RULES

---

## GHOST MODE

*For a first game, or when playing with new players.*

Null-Frames cannot collide with each other. Only wall and obstacle collisions apply.

This removes the chaos element entirely so new players can learn the Vector system and template selection without worrying about opponents. Recommended for the first race only.

## SLIPSTREAM

A Null-Frame directly behind another Null-Frame at the **same Altitude** and **same Velocity Vector** (within one Null-Frame length, not overlapping) may declare **Slipstream** at Step 1 of its activation.

A Slipstreaming Null-Frame may select movement templates as if it were one Vector higher. Its actual Vector does not change. No G-Force cost.

*This represents drafting off the Null-Frame ahead.*

## HYPE

Use the Gaslands: Refuelled Audience Votes system (Refuelled p. 88) as written, renamed **Hype**, with one restriction: any Hype effect that references weapons, attacks, Hull Points, or Crew is unavailable.

The most useful effects are Burn Rubber, Thunderous Applause, and Slippery Track. Others are at the referee's discretion.

> ##### IN DEVELOPMENT
> Hype is the last rule in G-Zero that requires the Gaslands: Refuelled book. A native replacement table is planned.

# PART VIII: WAYS TO PLAY

---

*G-Zero is a racing game first. Everything else is optional architecture built on top of that. Start with a Single Race. Add layers when the table is ready for them.*

## SINGLE RACE

One track. One result. Done.

Set up a circuit, place your Null-Frames on the starting grid, and race. All Null-Frames start at V1, Altitude Mid, Nerve 4. First pilot through the Finish Gate on the final lap wins.

Recommended for first games; use **Ghost Mode** to keep the focus on template selection and Vector management.

## TOURNAMENT

A series of Single Races with **no persistent state** between them. Pilots and frames reset completely between events. Score points per finishing position across a set number of events; most points at the end wins.

*Clean, competitive, no bookkeeping. The pilots do not carry their bruises. This is the format the Boards prefer for broadcast. Nothing inconvenient carries over.*

## SEASON

The full game. Pilots accumulate damage. Frames get destroyed. *The corp bleeds credits until it cannot field a legal entry, and then it is over.*

### THE ROSTER

Each corp fields between **1 and 3 Null-Frames** and up to **5 Pilots**.

A corp must field at least **1 Null-Frame with 1 Pilot** in every event or it is disqualified from that round. Miss enough rounds and the Boards revoke the licence.

*More pilots than frames means rotation is possible; it also means salary costs, competing agendas, and the question of who gets benched when the season gets ugly. Frames belong to the corp. Pilots belong to themselves, technically, though the contracts say otherwise.*

### RESOURCES

A single shared pool covers everything: frame acquisition and repair, entry fees, pilot contracts, medical, and training time. How the corp spends it is the season-level game.

> ##### IN DEVELOPMENT
> **Season economy is not yet costed.** Frame prices, entry fees, contract values, and per-event income are undefined. Until they are, agree a starting pool and price list at the table.

### FRAME LOSS

In Season play, a Dead Stick striking a wall or Heavy Solid Object uses this **expanded** table in place of the standard one, because a Season needs to know whether the frame is salvage or scrap:

- **1**: *The Emergency Dampeners fire their final function.* The frame bleeds velocity and stops. Remove it; it is **salvage**, not wreckage.
- **2 - 5**: The frame bounces. The nearest rival pilot pivots it to any facing. It continues activating each round at its last Vector, moving one Long Straight forward. *It will hit something again.*
- **6**: The frame is structurally compromised. Roll 2D6.
  - **Pair:** the frame is **destroyed**. Roll 1D6 to determine how: on 1-3 it folds into an **Alloy Pizza** (frame gone, no further effect); on 4-6 it **explodes** (frame gone; place a Large Burst Template centred on the point of impact and resolve immediately).
  - **Not a pair:** the frame shudders to a stop, as a result of 1.

A frame is also permanently lost when a pilot triggers **Red Mist**, or when a Dead Stick leaves the track boundary entirely.

Replacing a lost frame costs resources. A frame that stopped on the track rather than being destroyed may be recoverable between events, at the corp's expense and the referee's discretion.

### BETWEEN EVENTS

After each event, every pilot recovers **2 Nerve automatically**, regardless of what the corp does. Nerve cannot exceed 4.

Beyond that, the corp assigns each pilot one activity for the inter-event period:

| Assignment | Nerve Recovery | Other Effect |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| Resting | +2 | None |
| Training | +0 | Eligible for Feat progress |
| Medical | +1 | Clears one persistent condition or injury |
| "Dealt with" | - | Pilot leaves the roster |

A pilot who did not race recovers at the **Resting** rate by default unless the corp assigns them otherwise.

*Bench time is not free time; it is managed time. What pilots do with the hours the corp does not control is their own business, and occasionally the corp's problem.*

> ##### IN DEVELOPMENT
> Feat progression and the persistent condition / injury list are not yet written. See Part IX.

---

## RACE FORMATS

Race formats can be used in any mode: Single Race, Tournament, or Season.

### CIRCUIT

The standard format. A closed track with Gates at regular intervals. Null-Frames complete **3 laps** (adjustable); first through the Finish Gate wins. Positions behind first are recorded for points or campaign consequences.

### LONG TRACK

A single-run format on an open or linear course. No second lap; no recovery time. Pilots get one pass through the full track length. Finishing position is everything.

*The track does not forgive and does not repeat itself.*

### GATE HUNT

Gates are placed off the expected racing line, or in irregular sequences that require deliberate routing decisions. Passing a Gate out of sequence still does not count.

*The challenge is not raw speed but navigation under pressure: the fastest path is not always the correct one.*

### SPEED TRIAL

Scoring is based on peak velocity output across the event. Count the number of **Long Straight templates** each pilot places during the race; the pilot with the highest count wins, regardless of finishing position.

*A pilot who plays it safe at V3 all race and survives cannot win. A pilot who hits V6, triggers a Sonic Boom, and blacks out three times might. This format rewards aggression and punishes conservation; use it in a Season context and watch what it does to Nerve totals across the roster.*

## FEATS

*Feats are persistent upgrades a pilot learns, earns, and carries. They are not modifications to the shell; the shell is replaceable. A pilot who survives long enough to develop real instincts is considerably harder to manufacture. The Boards are aware of this. The pilots are not supposed to be.*

**Feats live and die with the pilot.** A dead pilot's Feats die with them.

*Nerve lost during a race does not simply return between races; a pilot who has been pushing their limits for three seasons feels it. What they gain instead is pattern recognition, timing, and the kind of calm that only comes from having already seen the worst the track can do.*

Feats fall into three categories:

- **Inherent** - things a pilot simply is. Neurology, physiology, the kind of talent that cannot be taught and is therefore deeply unfair.
- **Experience** - things a pilot has learned to do through repetition, consequence, and survival.
- **Implant** - purchased upgrades to the biological hardware; subscription-based in most enclaves, owned outright by almost nobody. Implants are the only Feats that can theoretically be transferred to another pilot. *The surgery required to do so is not covered by standard race insurance.*

Planned examples:

**Newtype** *(Inherent)* - Generate 1 additional Energy per activation, as if the Dampener field itself is half a second ahead of physics. *Whether this is genuine precognition, extreme sensory integration, or something the medical board does not have a classification for yet is not something the Boards encourage discussion of.*

**Iron Stomach** *(Inherent)* - The G-Load Check triggers at 7 G-Force tokens instead of 6. *The medical file attached to this Feat is three pages longer than standard and flagged for review after every race. The review always comes back clean.*

**Ghost Line** *(Experience)* - Once per race, change your Altitude after the Aggressor's Altitude is revealed in a collision. *They have seen this before. They already knew.*

**Hardwired Reflexes** *(Implant)* - Once per race, cancel all G-Force results from a single Nerve Dice roll after it is rolled. *The pilot's reaction latency has been surgically reduced to below 80 milliseconds.*

# QUICK REFERENCE

*Everything below is derived from the rules above. Where they disagree, the rules above are correct.*

## SETUP

All Null-Frames start at **V1**, **Altitude Mid**, **Nerve 4**, 0 G-Force. Standard race: **3 laps**.

## ROUND SEQUENCE

Vector Phases 1 to 6 in order. In each Phase: secret Altitude declaration, then activation in initiative order.
Qualified = current Vector equal to or higher than the Phase number. A frame activates once per round, in the Phase matching its Vector.
**Initiative order** = current race standing, leader first. Ties broken by distance to next Gate, then by roll-off.
**Leading Position** passes to the current race leader at the end of each round.
**End of round:** remove temporary Burst Templates.

## ACTIVATION SEQUENCE

| Step | Action |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | Declarations (Redout, Red Mist, Slipstream) |
| 2 | Reveal Altitude, resolve transition |
| 3 | Select and place template (pay High-G) |
| 4 | Roll Nerve Dice |
| 5 | Spend Energy |
| 6 | Move and resolve collisions |
| 7 | Lose unspent Energy; G-Load Check at 6+ G |

## NERVE DICE

Roll up to your current Nerve. Rolling is optional.

| Roll | Result |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 - 2 | +1 G-Force |
| 3 - 4 | Nothing |
| 5 - 6 | +1 Energy |

## SPENDING ENERGY

| Cost | Action |
|:---:|:---|
| 1 | Remove 1 G-Force (Inertial Dampener) |
| 1 | Shift Vector 1 step (+1 G per extra step) |
| 1 + 1 G | Drift: exit at the template's Drift point |
| 1 + 1 G | Spin: pivot up to 90 degrees at final position |

Vector range V1 to V6. Unspent Energy is lost at end of activation.

## ALTITUDE TRANSITIONS

| From | To Low | To Mid | To High |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Low** | +Short Straight, free | +2 Energy | 2 G first step, 1 G each further |
| **Mid** | +Medium Straight, 1 G | +1 Energy | 1 G first step, 1 G each further |
| **High** | +Long Straight, 2 G | +2 Energy | free first step, 1 G each further |

Going High lowers your Vector **for template selection only**.

## COLLISIONS

Collision Dice: **4, 5, 6 = success**. Aggressor = the frame that caused contact. Objects are always the Aggressor.

**Different Altitudes = Graze.** Winner rolls 3 dice, loser rolls 1.
High beats Mid. Mid beats Low. Low beats High.

**Same Altitude = Full Collision.** Both roll the same pool:

| Orientation | Pool |
|:---|:---|
| Head-On (front edge/corner) | Sum of both Vectors, min 3 |
| Broadside (side edge) | Higher Vector, min 2 |
| Tailgate (rear edge/corner) | Difference of Vectors, min 1 |

**Reactions:**

| Reaction | Who | Per success |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Brace | Anyone | +1 G on the other party, pivot them 30 degrees |
| Evade | Defender only | Cancel 1 G inflicted on you |

**Objects:** always Brace; all dice auto-succeed; the frame is Defender and may Evade; orientation does not apply.

| Object | Dice | Burst | After |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| Soft | 1 | Small | Destroyed; movement continues |
| Medium Solid | Your Vector | Large | Destroyed; -1 Vector |
| Heavy Solid | 2x your Vector | Extra-Large | Unaffected; movement stops |

## SONIC BOOM

First activation after switching to V6: place a Large Burst Template directly behind the frame, at start or end of movement (your choice). Removed at end of the Vector Phase.
Any other frame activating while overlapping it, or ending its activation overlapping it, rolls extra Nerve Dice equal to its current Nerve.

## G-LOAD CHECK

Triggered at **6+ G-Force at end of activation**. At Nerve 1, roll the Emergency Dampener Check first.

Roll dice equal to Nerve, minus 1 die per G beyond the sixth. Each die **equal to or above your current Vector** is a success.

| Successes | Result |
|:---:|:---|
| 3+ | **NERVES HOLD.** Nothing happens. |
| 2 | **NERVES FRAY.** Tumble; -1 Nerve. Vector kept. |
| 1 | **GREYOUT.** Override. Vector to V1, Altitude to Mid. |
| 0 | **BLACKOUT.** Tumble and Override; -1 Nerve. Vector to V1, Altitude to Mid. |

Discard all G-Force in every case.

## TUMBLE

1. **Direction:** away from the point of contact (collision) or the template centre (burst). No directional cause: 1D6 in six 60-degree wedges - 1 forward, 2 forward right, 3 back right, 4 back, 5 back left, 6 forward left.
2. **Distance:** Short (V1-V2), Medium (V3-V4), Long (V5-V6).
3. Frames in the path gain G equal to length: 1 / 2 / 3. No reactions, no Altitude check.
4. Nearest rival pivots the tumbling frame to any facing.
5. -1 Nerve, permanent (min 1).

## OVERRIDE AND RECOVERY

Override triggers on GREYOUT or BLACKOUT; the frame enters **Recovery** and skips its next qualifying activation. Does not stack.

## EMERGENCY DAMPENER CHECK

At Nerve 1 only, before any G-Load Check:

| Check | Survive on |
|:---:|:---:|
| 1st | 2+ |
| 2nd | 4+ |
| 3rd | 6 |
| 4th | Death, no roll |

Success: resolve the G-Load Check normally; a Tumble costs no further Nerve.
Failure: pilot dies; frame becomes a Dead Stick.

## DEAD STICK

Activates automatically in the Vector Phase matching its last Vector. Moves one Long Straight forward. No dice, no Altitude, no consequences. Gates do not count.
On hitting a wall or Heavy Solid: **1** stop and remove; **2-5** bounce, nearest rival pivots it; **6** removed, Large Burst at impact.
Leaving the track boundary removes it.

## PILOT ACTIONS

**Redout** - Start of activation. -1 Nerve permanently. Ignore all Vector restrictions on template selection this activation. Stackable. Not available at Nerve 1.

**Red Mist** - Once per race, start of activation, V5 or V6 only. Add two Long Straight templates in any order. No Nerve Dice, no self-inflicted G-Force. Pilot dies at end of activation; frame permanently lost. Recorded as a Finisher if the Finish Gate was crossed on the final lap. Not available at Nerve 1.
